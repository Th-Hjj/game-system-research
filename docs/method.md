# Research method

Start with the game and the question. Sketch an ordinary interaction from the player's goal through action to consequence. Identify actions, objects or state changes that still need explanation or evidence; investigate these gaps before peripheral details. Develop the sketch into a connected understanding of the system at the requested scope, rather than stopping once one ordinary interaction is explained.

Write for developers who have never played the game. Make a fully developed explanation the report's main body: how ordinary play unfolds, why the main rules matter together, and how outcomes change subsequent choices. Give these relationships enough space to be understood throughout the article, not just named in an introduction. Use examples, loops or diagrams when they support this explanation, not as replacements for it.

Verify evidence rigorously, then select report detail for understanding. Include conditions and exceptions whose omission would materially mislead; retain other findings in the evidence. Concision means avoiding repetition and irrelevant digressions, not shortening necessary explanation. Keep the structure flexible and distinguish supported consequences from claims about actual player experience or developer intent.

Review explanatory completeness and depth before trimming. The [Skill instructions](../skills/game-system-research/SKILL.md) define the workflow. [Scope and checks](validation.md) describe its limitations and review priorities.
