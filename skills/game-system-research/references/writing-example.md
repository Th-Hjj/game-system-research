# Selecting and connecting facts

This fictional example illustrates editorial choices, not researched game facts or a required report structure.

## Hypothetical system: Cave Salvage

Assume each expedition starts at the cave entrance with a full lamp. Moving between rooms consumes oil; each additional piece of treasure increases that cost. Returning to the entrance banks treasure. Running out of oil ends the expedition and loses carried treasure. Players can open shortcuts that persist between expeditions, including failed ones.

## Example passage

In Cave Salvage, you enter a cave with a full lamp, move from room to room, and bring treasure back to the entrance. Every doorway you cross uses oil. Picking up treasure makes subsequent crossings more expensive, so the route that brought you safely to a relic may leave you short of oil on the return journey. If the lamp runs dry before you return, the expedition ends and you lose all the treasure you are carrying.

Finding another relic therefore creates a practical decision. Taking it increases your haul, but also increases the oil needed to carry everything you already hold. You might collect it and head straight home, or leave it behind and keep exploring without adding to your load.

Opening a shortcut changes that calculation. Reaching it costs oil now, but its shorter route remains available even if this expedition ends with an empty lamp. On a later attempt, fewer crossings can leave more oil to bring treasure home. Exploration can improve future journeys as well as the current haul.

## Editorial choice

The passage answers successive questions: what the player does, why collecting treasure complicates the return, and how exploration changes later attempts. Each rule supports that explanation. Exact costs belong here if needed to answer the research question; details such as how shortcut states are stored remain in the evidence.
